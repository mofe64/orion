### Temporary physical pose runner

`orion-run-hardware-pose` was added as a guarded commissioning bridge until physical `ros2_control` exists. Its intended state sequence is:

```text
measured safe starting position
  -> named pose
  -> timed hold
  -> calibrated zero
  -> hold zero with monitoring until Ctrl+C
  -> captured torque-free rest pose
  -> disable torque
  -> operator turns 6 V off
```

Safety behaviour:

- It writes measured current positions as goals before enabling torque, preventing a jump to a stale target.
- It rejects starts or targets outside calibration and rejects raw 0/4095 wraparound crossings.
- It uses quintic position interpolation but only updates at 10 Hz (`0.10 s`).
- Current settings are shoulder torque `400/1000`, all other joints `200/1000`, `Goal_Velocity=50`, `Acceleration=5`.
- Pose cutoff is `50 C`; first-motion remains `45 C`.
- The `1 A` telemetry cutoff is a conservative commissioning guard, not the motor's rated or absolute current limit.
- A first Ctrl+C has graceful park-to-rest meaning only while already holding zero. Ctrl+C during movement, a second Ctrl+C during parking, terminal loss, a bus error, or another fault performs immediate torque-off. Because there are no passive brakes, emergency torque-off can cause collapse unless already at rest.

No complete named-pose cycle has passed on the real lamp yet.

## Bugs Fixed

1. **All servos initially appeared missing.** Once the 6 V rail and complete bus were correctly powered/connected, `/dev/ttyACM0` detected all five servos. This was hardware state, not an ID-map code defect.
2. **Calibration discarded the entire run when yaw exceeded the protected window.** Changed behaviour to retain measured travel but cap commandable base-yaw/head-roll limits at `+/-1004` raw and emit warnings.
3. **Stable rest pose was rejected by shared ranges.** Shoulder and elbow URDF/motion operational limits were deliberately expanded to include the physically captured stable pose.
4. **Power-off from zero would collapse the lamp.** Added a separate mechanically stable `rest` pose and graceful zero-to-rest shutdown transition before torque disable.
5. **Any Ctrl+C previously implied immediate torque-off.** During the zero-hold phase, the first Ctrl+C now requests a controlled park at rest; emergency interrupts during movement still disable immediately.
6. **Pose temperature failure did not identify the servo.** Pose health errors now include the hot joint name and temperature.

Unresolved failures:

- ID 3 elbow did not satisfy the strict first-nudge final tolerance even though it moved.
- The first `attentive` run with shoulder torque 300 failed with shoulder tracking error `740` raw steps.
- After shoulder torque was raised to 400 and the pose cutoff to 50 C, the shoulder moved sufficiently for the run to continue, but the elbow ended at raw `929` for target `606`: error `323` steps, reported phase peak current `228 mA`, maximum temperature `47 C`.
- Movement looked slow and jerky. The causes are the temporary 10 Hz user-space position stepping, low servo velocity/acceleration settings, low torque limits, and potentially load/model/trajectory mismatch. This is not representative of the intended 100 Hz ROS controller architecture.

## Key Decisions & Why

1. **Use calibration JSON rather than EEPROM for now.** It is reviewable, replaceable, backed up, and prevents premature persistent servo changes. EEPROM can be programmed later, but it is not required for the physical ROS adapter.
2. **Treat zero and rest as different states.** Zero is the kinematic reference used for pose angles; rest is the mechanically self-supporting shutdown configuration. Zero may require powered holding.
3. **Do not keep increasing torque blindly.** Tracking error can come from trajectory generation, an obstruction, supply sag, cable loading, thermal limiting, or insufficient torque. Raising the raw register without characterization can hide the cause and increase heating/damage risk.
4. **Move to a physical `ros2_control` backend.** The Python runner proved bus communication and safety sequencing, but it is an interim commissioning bridge. Runtime motion should go through the existing `joint_trajectory_controller` contract.
5. **Use simulation to estimate required joint torque, not final raw register values.** MuJoCo inverse dynamics can produce joint torque in N.m for static poses and trajectories. STS3215 `Torque_Limit` is not a calibrated N.m value, so simulation must be followed by cautious physical characterization.
6. **Use rated torque as the design reference, not stall torque.** Published STS3215 values at 6 V are approximately 0.39 N.m rated and 1.62 N.m stall. Stall is not a holding target.
7. **Keep graceful shutdown above the low-level hardware adapter.** A normal shutdown controller can command rest and then deactivate. A communication failure or emergency belongs in the adapter/watchdog and should torque off immediately.

## Lessons Learned & Gotchas

- Raw STS3215 encoder values are not joint angles. Convert relative to the captured neutral, apply the verified direction, and handle 4096-step wraparound explicitly.
- All captured `encoder_direction` values are currently `1`, inherited from LeLamp `drive_mode=0`, but those signs still need physical comparison with the URDF positive axes before general trajectory execution.
- The user manually edited shoulder and elbow calibration bounds on the Pi. The committed repository does not contain the calibration JSON. The adapter must not be built against the earlier pasted values.
- The current MuJoCo actuator default is uniformly capped at `-3.35..+3.35 N.m`, which is more than twice the published STS3215 6 V stall torque. Do not use that cap as evidence the real motors can execute a trajectory.
- URDF joint `effort="10"` values are generic placeholders, not actuator ratings.
- A current measurement of 228 mA during a failed trajectory does not prove adequate available torque; telemetry cadence, torque limiting, position-controller saturation, and the instant of peak load all matter.
- The 1 A cutoff is a software guard. Published figures are roughly 0.5 A rated current and 2 A stall current at 6 V, but actual safe continuous operation must be validated thermally in this assembly.
- Slow command duration alone does not guarantee smooth motion when the embedded velocity limit is approximately 4.4 degrees/s and commands arrive only at 10 Hz.
- Never support the powered arm with hands. Use padding/blocks while power is off, remove anything obstructing the planned path, keep hands clear, and keep the physical 6 V cutoff reachable.
- Never connect/disconnect the servo bus under motor power.
- Never cut 6 V expecting software to move to rest; software must reach rest before power is removed.

- Create an `orion_hardware` package with an `STS3215System` C++ `hardware_interface::SystemInterface` plugin.

- Export position commands and position/velocity state initially. Also read current, voltage, temperature, and status for diagnostics/watchdogs.

- On each write: enforce both calibrated hardware ranges and shared motion limits before converting radians to raw goals.
- On communication timeout, fault, thermal/current violation, or lifecycle error: stop writes and perform best-effort torque disable. Do not attempt a blind rest trajectory without reliable feedback.
- Add hardware-specific URDF `<ros2_control>` configuration and launch selection so `backend:=hardware` selects `orion_hardware/STS3215System` while Gazebo and MuJoCo retain their own adapters.
- Keep normal pose/motion execution in `joint_trajectory_controller`; do not place serial protocol logic in the controller.
- Implement a higher-level shutdown action/state machine: cancel current trajectory safely, command rest, confirm rest tolerance, deactivate/disable torque, then tell the operator it is safe to cut 6 V.
