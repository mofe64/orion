# Orion Studio design context

### Users

Orion Studio serves two overlapping audiences: developers and builders who
author, simulate, diagnose, and deploy character behaviour, and everyday Orion
owners who connect the robot, use voice, choose expressions, and understand its
live state. The primary jobs are creating and previewing poses, continuous
motions, lighting, sound, speech, and multimodal scenes; safely running those
assets on physical Orion; and diagnosing character state without needing to
understand low-level servo commands.

### Brand Personality

Calm, curious, and warm. Orion feels alive through restrained, purposeful expression rather than constant activity. Studio should evoke creative confidence and quiet delight: sophisticated enough for expert authoring, approachable enough that an owner can make Orion expressive without feeling as if they are operating industrial machinery.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use a refined dark creative-tool aesthetic rather than a generic engineering dashboard. Compose the workspace like an animation or sound-editing instrument: focused canvas, legible parallel tracks, precise inspectors, and progressive disclosure for advanced controls. Use the established navy surfaces with restrained blue, cyan, and violet accents, strong typography, and sparse spatial ornament inspired by Orion’s 8×5 light matrix. Avoid amber or cream interface palettes, neon cyberpunk styling, glassmorphism, generic card grids, dense terminal aesthetics, and consumer-toy visual language.

The interface is dark-mode-first. It must meet WCAG AA contrast, preserve visible keyboard focus, avoid color-only state communication, and respect reduced-motion preferences. Responsive layouts must retain critical authoring and robot-control functions rather than hiding them.

### Design Principles

1. **Expression first, hardware safely underneath.** Lead with poses, character states, scenes, timing, and previews; reveal calibration and diagnostics when they help a decision.
2. **One readable creative flow.** Selection, preview, editing, validation, and hardware playback should feel like one continuous workspace rather than separate tool panels.
3. **Calm hierarchy with precise feedback.** Use generous rhythm and restrained animation, while making run state, ownership, validation, and failure unmistakable.
4. **Progressive depth for two audiences.** Everyday controls remain obvious; advanced spline, marker, seed, calibration, and telemetry details stay available without dominating the default view.
5. **Accessible character, not decorative motion.** Meet WCAG AA, support keyboard and reduced-motion use, and use motion/light previews to communicate behavior rather than embellish chrome.
